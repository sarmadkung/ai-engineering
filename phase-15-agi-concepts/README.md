# Phase 15 — AGI Concepts

**Goal:** think clearly about what these systems do, what they don't, and where the field is going. This
phase is conceptual, but the questions are experiments you can actually run on your own — which is what
keeps it from being speculation.

**Why it's in a practical roadmap:** the gaps these debates concern are the same gaps you work around
every day — memory, verification, grounding, calibration. Reading the arguments tells you which of your
scaffolding is permanent and which is a temporary patch.

## Which things to learn

**56. Intelligence** — the competing definitions and what each implies; levels of generalization, and why
telling generalization from retrieval is hard; the kinds of reasoning and which are weak (causal,
counterfactual); the reversal curse; unfaithful chain of thought; in-context learning as an unplanned
surprise; and an honest list of what's missing.

**57. AGI Architectures** — what classical cognitive architectures got right; world models and the
evidence for and against LLMs having one; the agentic architecture you already built, mapped onto theory;
memory as a first-class component; **neural proposal plus symbolic verification** as the recurring winning
pattern; neurosymbolic, embodiment, scale maximalism; and which of your own scaffolding is permanent.

**58. AGI Research Topics** — scaling and its open questions; why sharp **emergence claims are often
metric artifacts**; RL for reasoning and where it's unproven; continual learning; embodiment; multimodality;
interpretability; and a method for following the field — replication, contamination awareness, and testing
claims on your own data.

**59. AGI Safety & Alignment** — outer versus inner alignment; specification gaming (which you've
probably already caused in your own evaluation harness); measurable sycophancy; why **capability
generalizes better than safety training**; corrigibility and its practical engineering form; governance and
what compliance will actually ask of you; present-day harms as distinct from long-term risk; and what a
practitioner should do differently.

## Prerequisites

Phases 1–14 give you the grounding to judge these claims. Access to models of different sizes helps;
local weights help for probing (Topic 59).

**Output:** two written position statements (Topics 56 and 59), dated, with falsifiable predictions and
your own experimental evidence. Revisit them in a year.
